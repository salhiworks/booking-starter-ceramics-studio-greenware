# Photographs

Fourteen frames from fourteen free photographs on Pexels, downloaded into the project (never
hot-linked), cropped for their one use, given one shared light grade and exported as AVIF +
WebP at three widths by the Greenware photo pipeline. **This starter ships only those processed
frames** — the original downloads and the pipeline script stay with the Niche Pack workspace
(`concepts/ceramics-studio-greenware/photo-pipeline/photos.mjs`), so the repository never
redistributes an unaltered copy. No image-generation tool was used: every pot, hand and shelf is
a real photograph, and nothing in any frame is retouched.

**One photographer, one shoot, one workshop.** All fourteen frames were published on Pexels by
**Yan Krukau** ([@yankrukov](https://www.pexels.com/@yankrukov/)) in a single series (Pexels ids
66112xx–66114xx, about 150 photographs) taken in one pottery workshop: big windows, white steel
shelving full of pots, a grey floor, dark canvas-topped tables on teal steel frames, a white
electric wheel, a teal banding wheel and one potter in a grey apron. The series covers every
story the site tells:

| On the page | Frame | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| First screen | `hero-centring` | From above, two hands centring a ball of clay on the wheel head |
| Wheel throwing | `wheel-throwing` | Hands drawing up the wall of a cylinder on the wheel |
| Handbuilding | `handbuilding` | Hands shaping a hand-pinched bowl on a banding wheel at the table |
| Your first lesson | `first-wedging` | The potter wedging clay on the canvas-topped table |
| Clay to kiln · 1 Wet clay | `stage-wet` | A freshly wedged lump of clay on the wheel head |
| Clay to kiln · 2 Leather-hard | `stage-leather` | A hand and a tool against a thrown pot on the wheel |
| Clay to kiln · 3 Bone dry | `stage-dry` | Unfired cups and a small vase drying along a radiator |
| Clay to kiln · 4 Bisque | `stage-bisque` | Small white unglazed hand-pinched bowls on a shelf |
| Clay to kiln · 5 Glazed | `stage-glazed` | Glazed ware on a wooden shelf: lilac dishes, white cups, a pink plate |
| Not every piece makes it | `process-crack` | An unfired bowl with cracks along its rim |
| The studio | `studio-window` · `studio-room` · `studio-tools` · `studio-shelves` | The windowsill of drying pieces; the workroom; tools in old mugs; finished ware on the shelves |

Using one shoot means the site shows **one physical workshop** in one light, instead of a collage
of unrelated craft stock. Group-class series (several students at several wheels), children's
classes and "family workshop" sets were looked at and set aside: they contradict a private,
one-to-one, adults-only studio. Other single frames (factory ceramics, glossy shop displays,
obviously staged hands) were also set aside.

**Honest captions.** The stage frames illustrate each stage; the captions and alt text describe
what the photograph actually shows (for example, stage 2 is described as a tool against a thrown
pot, not as a verified leather-hard piece). The pictured pottery is the photographed workshop's,
never students' work.

The shared grade is a touch less saturation and gently lifted shadows, so the cool workshop light
sits calmly on the bisque-white page.

They are the website's **defaults** (`src/content/site.ts`): the owner replaces each one in the
dashboard (Website). **They are not Turning Room's teacher, students, work or studio** (Turning
Room is fictional): the potter did not teach there, is not named, and nothing implies he endorses
it. On the page:

- every photo carries a **"Sample"** label until the owner replaces that field;
- the studio section says the photographs are licensed sample images of **another pottery
  workshop — not this studio, its teacher or its students** (and, in the Live Demo and preview,
  that the studio itself is fictional).

## Replacing them

Upload your own in the dashboard → Website. Photos of your own studio, your hands and your own
pieces read best; ask permission before showing a student or their work. Keep the clay and hands
away from the edges of the first-screen photo — it is cropped differently on phones.
